AI GOVERNANCE

AI governance & RegTech

Overview

A vendor model can appear healthy even when its inputs are stale or its outputs are unreliable for one customer segment. Define the decision it supports and the consequence of an error before deciding how much validation it needs.

Approval is specific to a use, a version and a set of conditions. Feed failures, performance drift and material changes should lead to an accountable decision about fallback, revalidation or retirement.

Partner handoff

Ask the vendor for the affected versions and a change log. An independent advisor may challenge the validation design. The institution’s approval authority decides whether the service can resume.

Before relying on a black-box score, how can an institution show that it works for its customers, channels and intended use?

A vendor assurance report can support specified security controls. Test detection separately using known positive and negative examples, feed freshness, subgroup performance and version changes. Provide independent challenge.

Decision trade-off

A model closer to a block, refusal or filing decision has greater consequences. Tier approvals and fallbacks differently for summarization, prioritization and consequential uses.

Research themes

Match validation to the use case

Separate policy summaries, case drafting, risk scoring and decisions that affect customers. Validation should examine inputs, foreseeable failures, human override and the consequences for a customer when the output is wrong.

Testing a proprietary model

Even when source code is proprietary, an institution can test known cases and counterexamples, obtain version and provenance records, and agree on change notices and suspension triggers. Restrict the tool’s use if material discrepancies remain unexplained.

Options for overseeing RegTech

Vendor questionnaires, independent assurance and closer oversight of critical services offer different costs and safeguards. Compare substitutability, concentration across institutions and the effect of an outage on reporting. These are options for policy design.

Who needs an AI disclosure?

Identify the party supplying the generation tool, the party publishing its output and the audience. Establish the content type, use and jurisdiction before deciding whether machine-readable marking, an audience disclosure or another control fits the obligation.

From risk to control and evidence

Test the decision a tool supports, the inputs it needs and the consequences of a wrong answer. A functioning interface can conceal stale data or a change in model behaviour.

How to measure effectiveness

Time from a missed or faulty input to detection and verified restoration. Report the affected population alongside that interval.

Case analysis

Illustrative scenario

A screening feed stops updating while the system shows healthy status. Compare expected and actual delivery, scope the affected window, activate an approved fallback, rescreen and preserve exceptions and independent validation.

Official references

  • OSFI

    Guideline E-23 · Model Risk Management (2027)

    The revised guideline takes effect on 1 May 2027 for federally regulated financial institutions within its scope. It ties how models are reviewed, approved and monitored to their inherent risk.

    Open official source
  • European Commission

    AI Act Article 50 · Transparency duties

    The Commission explains the duties that apply from 2 August 2026. Providers may have to mark generated content in a machine-readable format, and deployers may have to disclose when they present deepfakes or other covered content.

    Open official source
