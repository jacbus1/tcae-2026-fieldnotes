RISK GOVERNANCE

Boards & risk governance

Overview

A growing alert queue can signal too few investigators, poor rule performance or a broken data feed. Management needs the cause and the size of the exposure before it can compare staffing, rule changes and a data repair.

Present the options with their trade-offs. Record who accepted the residual risk, what interim control covers it, when directors will review it again, and what evidence would change the decision.

How should a board choose between more investigators, a data repair and a change to monitoring rules?

The same backlog can arise from capacity limits, unproductive alerts or missing data. Establish the cause, then compare cost, exposure window, ownership and acceptance evidence on one page.

Decision trade-off

More staff can shorten a queue while transactions missing from intake stay unseen. Name separate owners for interim coverage and the longer-term data fix.

Research themes

What decision does the board need to make?

Put the exposure, affected business, risk tolerance and requested decision on the first page. For each option, show cost, delivery time and remaining risk. After the decision, record the owner and next review date.

Comparing prevention and remediation costs

Estimate preventive investment from the volume of high-risk cases requiring review and available capacity. Remediation can involve lookbacks, outside support and service restrictions. Show the scope and assumptions that produce the range.

Governing private-to-private sharing

Before a private exchange, establish the mechanism, eligibility, purpose and information permitted. Retain the basis, recipient and approval for the disclosure and track use restrictions. Regulatory reporting and disclosures to law enforcement each have their own authority.

Translating regulatory change into action

For each change, record the jurisdiction, official text, commencement status, affected entity and owner. Use different case exercises for directors and front-line teams to test whether they can identify when the new requirements call for escalation.

From risk to control and evidence

Give decision-makers the cause, exposure and options behind a risk indicator. A temporary workaround needs a capacity estimate, an expiry date and a clear return to normal control.

How to measure effectiveness

Share of open high-risk issues past their approved review date. Show the age and residual exposure of each overdue issue.

Case analysis

Illustrative scenario

Overdue high-risk cases coincide with a partial new-channel feed. Management presents the denominator and impact for each, then proposes interim manual review, a feed repair and a time-bound independent retest.
