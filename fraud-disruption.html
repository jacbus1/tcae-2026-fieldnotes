FRAUD DISRUPTION

Fraud disruption & law enforcement

Overview

A credible scam signal creates two clocks. The fraud team may have minutes to protect a customer before a payment becomes irreversible. The AML team evaluates the transaction history, relevant parties and grounds for reporting under its own process.

Track each outcome separately. A warning, a hold, funds recovered and money returned to a victim describe different events. Link them to the case to avoid counting the same funds more than once.

Partner handoff

A designated liaison can coordinate an actionable lead with a financial crime task force or law-enforcement partner under the applicable authority. Fraud operations follows up with the customer while the AML team assesses reporting.

Between the first scam signal and irreversible payment, who can intervene lawfully and who owns subsequent intelligence and evidence?

Fraud teams address immediate harm while AML staff independently assess suspicious flows. Prevention, holds, recovery, return and criminal outcomes are distinct measures with different denominators and time windows.

Decision trade-off

Timely cross-sector signals can protect customers. Operational intelligence may still lack evidential provenance or process, so establish purpose, authority and minimum necessary disclosure.

Research themes

Parallel fraud and AML decisions

While a payment may still be interruptible, identify who is authorized to contact the victim, platform or receiving institution. Preserve the timeline in parallel so AML staff can assess reporting and disclosure obligations independently.

From a shared lead to usable intelligence

When an outside party shares a phone number, account or wallet lead, record its source, timing and restrictions. Look for matching internal activity and state confidence in the identity link. Information exchange depends on an authorized route and its disclosure conditions.

Making sense of disruption outcomes

Count victim contacts, blocked transfers, frozen funds, recoveries and returns separately. One case may involve several interventions. A success rate requires a defined denominator and records that distinguish interventions from unique cases.

Protecting a victim after a deceptive authorization

A customer may personally authorize a transaction or wallet permission while misunderstanding its scope. Ask whom the customer intended to pay and why, reconcile the timing and subsequent movement, and choose an intervention suited to the customer’s exposure.

From risk to control and evidence

Connect an urgent fraud signal to the team that can still act on the payment. Track customer protection, reporting decisions and final financial outcomes separately.

How to measure effectiveness

Median time from verified signal to authorized action for payments still within an intervention window. Report customer returns separately from amounts temporarily held.
