PUBLIC REFERENCES

Public references and reading notes

This independent guide draws on the TCAE Toronto conference. The frameworks and illustrative scenarios are editorial applications. Use the official sources below to check jurisdiction, scope and effective dates.

FINTRAC

Examination methods and sampling

Examiners can compare policies, staff interviews, sampled transactions and reports.

FINTRAC

Reporting thresholds and deadlines

A completed or attempted transaction meeting the suspicion threshold for a specified offence must be reported as soon as practicable.

Read official guidance
FINTRAC

Voluntary private-to-private sharing

Participants act under the same privacy-commissioner-approved code and must assess the conditions for each disclosure.

Read official guidance
Bank of Canada

Stablecoin issuer framework

The Bank highlights high-quality liquid reserves and par redemption. Implementing regulations were published as proposals in Canada Gazette Part I.

Read official guidance
Bank of Canada

Retail payments supervisory framework

Assess payment functions, geographic scope and exclusions. Digital currencies are excluded from the framework’s EFT currency test. Assess the fiat leg separately.

Read official guidance
OSFI

E-23 model risk management

The revised guideline takes effect on 1 May 2027 for in-scope federally regulated financial institutions. Scale review, monitoring and approval to inherent model risk.

Read official guidance
European Commission

AI Act Article 50 transparency

From 2 August 2026, duties depend on the role and use case. Provider marking and deployer disclosures have distinct conditions.

Read official guidance
OSFI / FINTRAC

Supervisory mandates

OSFI is the prudential regulator. FINTRAC is Canada’s financial intelligence unit and AML/ATF supervisor.

Read official guidance
iGaming Ontario

Entering the Ontario market

Ontario’s market-entry process distinguishes AGCO registration from executing an operating agreement with iGaming Ontario. Check the entity and jurisdiction when reviewing an operator.

Read official guidance
Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre

Maple Disruption 2.0

The November 2026 operation targeted communications and financial services used to enable fraud. Intervention counts and amounts returned to victims measure different outcomes.
