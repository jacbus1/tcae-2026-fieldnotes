PUBLIC REFERENCES

Public references and reading notes

This independent guide draws on the TCAE Toronto conference. The frameworks and illustrative scenarios are editorial applications. Use the official sources below to check jurisdiction, scope and effective dates.

  • FINTRAC

    Assessment manual for compliance examinations

    The manual follows an examination from scoping to findings. It explains how examiners use interviews, policies and transaction samples to test whether controls operate in practice.

    Open official source
  • FINTRAC

    Financial transaction reporting thresholds and deadlines

    FINTRAC lists the triggers and deadlines by report type. For completed or attempted transactions, a suspicious transaction report is due as soon as practicable once there are reasonable grounds to suspect money laundering, terrorist financing or sanctions evasion.

    Open official source
  • FINTRAC

    Private-to-private information sharing

    Reporting entities can share personal information under an approved code of practice. The guidance sets out who can participate and what conditions apply to each disclosure.

    Open official source
  • Bank of Canada

    Stablecoin issuer framework

    The Bank’s supervision framework for fiat-backed stablecoins focuses on high-quality liquid reserves and redemption at face value. Draft implementing regulations were published in Part I of the Canada Gazette.

    Open official source
  • Bank of Canada

    Retail payments supervisory framework

    This guide explains how payment functions, geographic scope and exclusions determine who must register. The test for electronic funds transfers covers Canadian and foreign currencies and excludes digital currencies.

    Open official source
  • OSFI

    Guideline E-23 · Model Risk Management (2027)

    The revised guideline takes effect on 1 May 2027 for federally regulated financial institutions within its scope. It ties how models are reviewed, approved and monitored to their inherent risk.

    Open official source
  • European Commission

    AI Act Article 50 · Transparency duties

    The Commission explains the duties that apply from 2 August 2026. Providers may have to mark generated content in a machine-readable format, and deployers may have to disclose when they present deepfakes or other covered content.

    Open official source
  • OSFI

    How OSFI and FINTRAC work together

    OSFI supervises the financial soundness of federally regulated institutions, and FINTRAC oversees anti-money laundering and anti-terrorist financing compliance. The page explains how the agencies share relevant supervisory information.

    Open official source
  • iGaming Ontario

    Steps to Join the Ontario Market

    Online gaming operators entering Ontario must complete steps with two bodies. The guide lists what operators need for AGCO registration and for an operating agreement with iGaming Ontario.

    Open official source
  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre

    Maple Disruption 2.0

    The November 2026 operation brought together police, financial institutions and other partners to disrupt services used in fraud. The report gives separate figures for disruptive actions, reported losses and money recovered.

    Open official source
